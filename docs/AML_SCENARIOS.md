# AML lab — scenarios and rules

> Generated by `npm run seed`. Dataset seed 20260630. Fictional data; teaching notes are discussion guidance, not ground truth.

## Rules

| ID | Name | Category | Weight | Logic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `R1_HIGH_VELOCITY` | Transaction frequency anomaly | Velocity | 25 | Count of transactions in any rolling 7-day window ≥ max(10, 3 × expected weekly count). Expected weekly = declared monthly count ÷ 4.33. |
| `R2_LARGE_VS_PROFILE` | Unusual amount vs. profile | Amount | 30 | Single transaction ≥ 3 × declared monthly turnover AND ≥ $10,000. |
| `R3_CASH_JUST_BELOW_THRESHOLD` | Repeated cash just below $10,000 | Cash | 45 | ≥ 3 cash deposits each between $9,000 and $9,999.99 within 7 days. |
| `R4_INFLOW_EXCEEDS_PROFILE` | Monthly inflow exceeds profile | Profile | 20 | Total credits in a calendar month ≥ 3 × declared monthly turnover. |
| `R5_DUPLICATE_POSTING` | Possible duplicate posting | Data quality | 15 | Two or more transactions on the same account with identical reference, amount, direction and counterparty within 1 day. |
| `R6_RAPID_PASS_THROUGH` | Rapid pass-through of funds | Movement | 35 | Credit ≥ $10,000 followed within 2 days by outgoing wires to other counterparties totalling ≥ 90% of the credit. |
| `R7_PROFILE_INCONSISTENCY` | Activity inconsistent with declared purpose | Profile | 30 | Declared purpose excludes international payments AND incoming international wires from ≥ 3 distinct counterparties within 30 days. |

## Scenarios

### S1 — Transaction frequency anomaly

- Customer: CUS-1007 · Case: CASE-260101 · Score: 35 (Medium)
- Rules: R1_HIGH_VELOCITY
- Pattern: A small printing business with ~12 transactions a month receives 16 incoming EFTs from 14 different payers in six days.
- Suggested proportionate step: REQUEST_INFORMATION
- Teaching note: The burst is far above the declared pattern, but the individual amounts are modest and consistent with customer payments. Nothing on file explains it, and KYC is overdue for refresh. A proportionate next step is a request for information (e.g. invoices or a description of the job driving the payments) combined with the KYC refresh — not immediate escalation, and not closure without support.

### S2 — Unusual amount vs. profile

- Customer: CUS-1012 · Case: CASE-260105 · Score: 50 (Medium)
- Rules: R2_LARGE_VS_PROFILE, R4_INFLOW_EXCEEDS_PROFILE
- Pattern: A salaried retail customer with ~$6.5k monthly credits receives a single $48,500 domestic wire from a title/escrow company.
- Suggested proportionate step: REQUEST_INFORMATION
- Teaching note: A one-off large credit from an escrow-type counterparty is consistent with a property sale, and a KYC note mentions a planned sale — but nothing is documented. The note makes the explanation plausible, not proven. Requesting the closing statement is the evidence-led step before deciding.

### S3 — Customer profile inconsistency

- Customer: CUS-1019 · Case: CASE-260108 · Score: 100 (High)
- Rules: R2_LARGE_VS_PROFILE, R4_INFLOW_EXCEEDS_PROFILE, R6_RAPID_PASS_THROUGH, R7_PROFILE_INCONSISTENCY
- Pattern: A student account declared for payroll deposits receives four international wires from four unrelated companies, each moved out within ~1 day.
- Suggested proportionate step: ESCALATE_TO_COMPLIANCE
- Teaching note: Three independent indicators align: activity type inconsistent with the declared purpose, volume far above profile, and funds passing straight through. There is no documented explanation and the counterparties have no apparent link to the customer. This warrants escalation to compliance for their assessment; the simulation does not — and cannot — conclude that an offence occurred.

### S4 — Duplicate source / ERP entries

- Customer: CUS-1024 · Case: CASE-260102 · Score: 20 (Low)
- Rules: R5_DUPLICATE_POSTING
- Pattern: Three supplier receipts appear twice in May. Each pair has identical reference, amount and date but different migration load runs.
- Suggested proportionate step: CLOSE_EXPLAINED_EXCEPTION
- Teaching note: The duplicates differ only by load run (1 vs 2) in batch MIG-0612, a known defect (re-run without idempotent load) is logged, and the reconciliation report confirms the extra rows. Removing them, no rule fires. Closing as an explained data exception is supported — with a data-correction request so the ledger is fixed at source.

### S5 — Suspicious-looking migration defect

- Customer: CUS-1031 · Case: CASE-260103 · Score: 55 (Medium)
- Rules: R2_LARGE_VS_PROFILE, R4_INFLOW_EXCEEDS_PROFILE
- Pattern: A $1,245,000.00 receipt for a farm-supply company — ~7× its declared monthly turnover in one transaction. The source ERP shows $12,450.00 for the same reference.
- Suggested proportionate step: CLOSE_EXPLAINED_EXCEPTION
- Teaching note: The migrated amount is exactly 100× the source document, the row comes from batch MIG-0612 with a logged amount-scale defect, and reconciliation confirms the mismatch. Re-evaluating with the source amount, no rule fires. Closing as an explained data exception is supported for THIS transaction; the defect does not explain any other activity, which should still be judged on its own evidence.

### S6 — Unresolved exception requiring escalation

- Customer: CUS-1015 · Case: CASE-260107 · Score: 95 (High)
- Rules: R2_LARGE_VS_PROFILE, R3_CASH_JUST_BELOW_THRESHOLD, R4_INFLOW_EXCEEDS_PROFILE
- Pattern: Four cash deposits between $9,400 and $9,900 at three branches in five days, followed by a $37,000 wire out. The customer has not answered a request for information.
- Suggested proportionate step: ESCALATE_TO_COMPLIANCE
- Teaching note: Repeated cash just under $10,000 across branches, inflow far above profile, rapid outbound wire, and no response to the RFI after four weeks. No data-quality explanation exists (all rows are live core transactions). The exception remains unresolved, so escalation to compliance is the appropriate outcome. Any reporting decision belongs to the compliance function, not to this tool.

### S7 — Rapid pass-through of funds

- Customer: CUS-1027 · Case: CASE-260106 · Score: 45 (Medium)
- Rules: R6_RAPID_PASS_THROUGH
- Pattern: An import/export company receives $85,000 and sends $79,500 out in two wires within 30 hours, one to a new counterparty.
- Suggested proportionate step: FURTHER_INVESTIGATION
- Teaching note: Paying suppliers and freight forwarders soon after receiving customer funds can be normal for a trading company, and international payments are part of the declared profile. But one recipient is new and the speed is notable. Further investigation (counterparty research, invoices, past pattern) is proportionate before any decision.

### S8 — Documented seasonal spike

- Customer: CUS-1003 · Case: CASE-260104 · Score: 20 (Low)
- Rules: R4_INFLOW_EXCEEDS_PROFILE
- Pattern: A landscaping company’s May inflows are more than 3× its declared monthly average. Seasonality is documented in the KYC file.
- Suggested proportionate step: CLOSE_EXPLAINED_EXCEPTION
- Teaching note: The only indicator is volume, the counterparties look like residential and property-management clients, and the KYC file documents April–June seasonality with prior-year statements. Closing as an explained exception is supported, and the customer profile could be updated to reflect seasonality so the alert does not recur.
